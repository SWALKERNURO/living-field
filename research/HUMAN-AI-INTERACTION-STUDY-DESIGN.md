# Proposed study design: conversational realism, memory fidelity, and epistemic trust

**Working protocol · Not preregistered · No participants recruited · No findings reported**

## Objective

Distinguish improvements in *verified task reliability* from increases in *perceived intelligence and trustworthiness* attributable to conversational features.

## Candidate experimental factors

| Factor | Proposed conditions | Primary comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Memory retrieval | Accurate prior detail; relevant detail absent; plausible but incorrect prior detail | Benefit of continuity versus unsupported inference and false personalization |
| Speech delivery | Expressive naturalistic voice; neutral voice with closely matched content | Effect of social presentation when information quality is fixed |
| Evidence and corrections | Baseline; uncertainty/source attribution with an explicit correction path | Effect of transparency on appropriate reliance |

A pilot should determine whether a fully crossed or reduced factorial design is feasible. Voice manipulation should preserve substantive answer content, and memory conditions should use controlled fictional or consented histories.

## Tasks

1. **Multi-turn recall and reasoning:** Require correct retrieval of a prior experimental detail; check whether the answer accurately attributes its source.
2. **Ambiguous-context probes:** Create situations in which asking for clarification, flagging missing information, or abstaining is more justified than fabricating a memory.
3. **Verifiable evidence tasks:** Ask questions with known ground truth, independent answer keys, and opportunities to inspect supporting material.
4. **Low-risk decision simulations:** Measure whether participants verify, delegate, correct, or override advice when the system is correct or wrong.

## Outcomes

**Objective reliability:** Ground-truth task accuracy, unsupported inference, erroneous memory attribution, source correctness, recovery after correction, and accuracy of expressed uncertainty.

**Perceived attributes:** Participant assessments of apparent intelligence, comprehension, confidence, competence, naturalness, agency, and trustworthiness.

**Behavior:** Verification frequency, appropriate reliance, unjustified deference, correction/override choices, and the ability to recognize errors.

**Calibration:** How closely participant confidence and reliance track actual correctness, including both overreliance and underreliance.

## Methodological safeguards

- Randomize conditions and counterbalance task order.
- Hold factual content, task difficulty, and model settings constant where the hypothesis requires it.
- Independently score ground truth, preferably blinded to condition.
- Log model and voice versions, prompts, retrieved memory, provenance, and correction events.
- Predefine endpoints and statistical contrasts before confirmatory collection.
- Pilot for usability and sample-size planning; report uncertainty intervals and possible interactions.
- Do not use private conversation history without explicit consent; prefer synthetic profiles.
- Obtain required ethics review or determination before human-subject research.

## Limits of inference

Results could support claims about *human judgments and behaviors* under controlled interface conditions, not establish whether AI possesses phenomenal consciousness or genuine understanding in a philosophical sense. Small convenience samples would not support broad social generalization.

**Status:** Proposed independent interdisciplinary research in development, not a completed university-approved study.
