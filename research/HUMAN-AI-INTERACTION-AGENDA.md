# Research agenda: the cognitive and philosophical foundations of human–AI interaction

**Spencer H. Walker** · University of Denver, undergraduate philosophy studies  
**Status:** Independent proposal in development; not a completed or institutionally sponsored study

## Central question

> How do persistent conversational memory, naturalistic speech, and contextual inference affect the difference between an AI system's actual reliability and a user's perception of its intelligence and trustworthiness?

## Rationale

Conversational systems retrieve earlier information, use naturalistic speech, and make contextual inferences across interactions. These capabilities may make them more effective collaborators. But they may also lead users to mistake interactional fluency for knowledge, genuine comprehension, or reliable reasoning. An assistant may appear attentive while relying on an erroneous memory or an inference with no adequate evidentiary support.

The philosophical distinction is between **appearance and epistemic warrant**: what users experience as understanding versus what observable evidence justifies believing about a system's competence. The empirical question is whether interface and memory choices affect actual results, perceived intelligence, and users' decisions to rely on the system.

## Hypotheses

**H1 — Memory fidelity.** Correct, task-relevant memory retrieval improves continuity and task success. Missing, incorrectly attributed, or misleading memory increases false personalization and unsupported claims.

**H2 — Expressive delivery.** With answer content and objective accuracy held constant, naturalistic expressive speech increases perceived intelligence, competence, or willingness to rely, compared with neutral delivery.

**H3 — Epistemic transparency.** Showing uncertainty, source provenance for retrieved claims, and actionable correction controls improves the alignment of confidence and reliance with measured accuracy, especially after an error.

## Philosophical and information-science implications

- **Other minds and machine understanding:** Behavioral cues of understanding are not equivalent to proof of understanding or consciousness.
- **Epistemic autonomy:** Delegation can improve productivity while also redistributing authority and weakening independent checking.
- **Personal continuity and memory:** Accurate retrieval of past conversations does not by itself establish personal identity or persistent subjective experience.
- **Language and social cognition:** Voice, naturalism, politeness, and conversational style can create credible social signals even when factual reliability is unchanged.
- **Governance and accountability:** A trustworthy agent should make the limits of memory, inference, source evidence, and authorized actions contestable by the user.

## Intended contribution

A testable account of *trust calibration* that keeps three outcomes separate:

1. **System performance:** correctness, source grounding, retrieval precision, unsupported-inference rate, and recovery from error.
2. **User perception:** apparent intelligence, understanding, competence, intentionality, and trustworthiness.
3. **User behavior:** verification, reliance, delegation, correction, override, and ability to recognize errors.

The goal is to identify when conversational realism enables effective collaboration and when it contributes to **unwarranted deference or anthropomorphism**.

## Boundaries

No human-subject study results, causal estimates, ethics approval, or university endorsement are claimed here. A final protocol would require appropriate ethical review or determination, careful experimental controls, defined outcome measures, and analysis suited to the sample.
